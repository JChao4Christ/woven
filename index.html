The Passage

12Put on then, as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience, 13bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive. 14And above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony.
Colossians 3:1–17 · ESV

Gospel Reflection — read this to each other

Justin and Vanessa, as beloved children of God, you have been clothed in Christ. This passage isn't a new set of rules to try harder to obey, but a beautiful description of what flows naturally from your new identity in Him. You are already chosen, holy, and beloved. This is the bedrock. From this secure place, Paul invites you to put on virtues like compassion, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience.

Consider the grace extended to you in Christ. He bore with your failings, not just a few times, but to the cross, forgiving a debt you could never repay. This limitless, unconditional forgiveness is the wellspring for how you are called to bear with one another. When the inevitable friction of life, parenthood, or ministry arises—a missed cue, an unsaid word, a moment of impatience—remember that you both stand forgiven. Your marriage is not a ledger of wrongs, but a testament to Christ's ongoing, active grace.

The call to “forgive each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive” (v. 13) is profound. It means your forgiveness of Vanessa, Justin, and your forgiveness of Justin, Vanessa, isn't about their deserving it, but about mirroring the divine heart that has already forgiven you. This isn't easy; it takes a supernatural grace. But because you are united with Christ, His Spirit empowers you to choose compassion over complaint, and grace over grievance.

Finally, “above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony” (v. 14). This love, agape, is not merely a feeling, but a costly, self-giving action, perfected in Christ's sacrifice. It’s the unifying thread in your lives, in your partnership, and in your family with Abigail. This week, may you lean into the reality that the love you share is a reflection of the greater love that holds you fast—a love freely given, not earned, and always repairing what is broken by human failing.

A note from the original

χαριζόμενοι — charizomenoi (forgiving) — from χάρις (charis, grace) The Greek word for “forgiving” in Colossians 3:13 comes from “grace.” It implies a gracious, free act of pardoning, not based on merit or obligation, but mirroring God's undeserved kindness to us in Christ. This echo of grace is foundational to gospel-centered forgiveness within marriage.

Bring It Into Your Marriage

For JustinYour Worth, Not Your Output

Justin, your drive to build and ship, whether for income or ministry, is a good gift. Yet, the pressure to produce can subtly whisper that your worth is tied to your output. This week's passage reminds you that before you *do* anything, you *are* “chosen of God, holy and beloved.” Your identity is settled, not by the success of SimplyAI4U or the reach of your AI ministry, but by the finished work of Christ. When frustrations arise—a coding block, a missed deadline, or even a moment of impatience with Vanessa or Abigail—remember that you are already forgiven, already accepted. Let this profound truth free you to offer compassion and forgiveness, not as another item on a to-do list, but as an overflow of Christ's grace in you, resting in His love rather than striving for yours.

For PaolaBearing with Grace

Vanessa, in the rhythms of your days—whether nurturing Abigail, supporting Justin's calling, or managing the home—there are countless opportunities for patience to be tested or for a “complaint against another” to arise. This passage invites you to see these moments not as obstacles, but as invitations to lean into the grace already poured out for you. You are “chosen of God, holy and beloved”—a co-heir with Justin, dignified and cherished. What specific burdens or frustrations are you bearing in your heart right now? This week, reflect on how Christ has borne your burdens and forgiven you. How might His grace empower you to bear with Justin, or extend forgiveness in a specific area, not out of duty, but as a reflection of your secure identity in Him? Speak to the Lord in your own words about this, and consider how you might embody this compassionate heart.

For UsA Household of Repair

Justin and Vanessa, Abigail is watching. Not just your disagreements, but also—and perhaps more importantly—how you repair them. Your home is a training ground for grace, a place where compassion, forgiveness, and love are not just spoken of, but lived out daily. This week, consider how you can intentionally model this “household of repair” for Abigail. When you inevitably stumble in word or deed with each other, see it as an opportunity to demonstrate the kind of grace Christ has shown you. Can you commit to not letting a “complaint” linger, but instead actively seek to forgive and restore, knowing that love “binds everything together in perfect harmony”? Let your love for each other, rooted in Christ, be a visible testimony to your daughter of the gospel's power to reconcile and renew.

Talk It Through — together

If any question turns into an argument: stop mid-sentence, hold hands, one of you prays one sentence, and park the topic in the notes box for your Covenant check-in or a walk this week. Finishing the fight is not tonight's assignment — the cord holds even when the conversation pauses. Either of you can call this, no explanation owed.

Take sixty seconds of quiet before the tender ones — think before either of you speaks. Whoever didn't read the section aloud answers first.

  1. Share a story from your week where you felt particularly grateful for something the other did or said, even a small gesture. What made that moment meaningful to you?
  2. Colossians 3:12 says we are to put on “compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience.” Which of these virtues feels most challenging for you to “put on” in your marriage right now, and why do you think that might be?
  3. Verse 13 speaks of “bearing with one another.” In what specific situations do you find yourselves needing to bear with each other most often? How does remembering Christ's bearing with you change your perspective on these moments?
  4. The passage directly commands, “as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive.” Without focusing on specific past hurts unless you both feel ready, what does it mean for your marriage to let Christ’s forgiveness *for you* be the engine of your forgiveness *for each other*?
  5. Verse 14 culminates with love binding everything in “perfect harmony.” What is one small, tangible way you can demonstrate this kind of unifying love to each other or to Abigail this week, as co-heirs of God’s grace?
One ask each: before you close this, each of you name one concrete thing you'd like from the other this week. Frame it as a please-do, not a please-stop, and make it small enough to actually happen this week. Say it kindly; receive it without defending. If an ask feels too big to promise, say so gently — an ask is a gift requested, not a debt incurred. Write both below so you remember.
